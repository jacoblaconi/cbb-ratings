Method

Ratings follow KenPom's published method. Where KenPom doesn't publish a detail, we use Torvik's documented choice and tune it on past seasons.

Ratings

  1. Possessions per game: FGA − ORB + TO + 0.475 × FTA, averaged over both teams. Only games between two D-I teams count.
  2. Raw efficiency: points scored (OE) and allowed (DE) per 100 possessions. Tempo: possessions per 40 minutes.
  3. Adjusted efficiency: an additive model, as KenPom uses now. Each game's offensive efficiency is modelled as national average + the team's offense term + the opponent's defense term, with home court removed. All teams are solved together by weighted least squares.
  4. Home court: a flat 3.75 points per game.
  5. AdjT: tempo adjusted for opponents the same way, in possessions per 40 minutes against an average-tempo team.
  6. AdjEM = AdjO − AdjD, the margin per 100 possessions against an average D-I team.
  7. Recency: games count fully for 40 days, then 1% less per day, down to 60% at 80 days.
  8. Blowouts: efficiency margins beyond 40 points per 100 possessions count at half their size.
  9. Preseason prior: a roster-based projection worth 6 games of data, fading to zero by a team's 18th game. It starts from last season's rating, scaled by the share of last season's production that returns. Incoming transfers add value based on how much they produced at their old D-I school and how good that team was. Freshmen and other newcomers count at an average newcomer level. Teams new to Division I start a little below average (−6 AdjEM) plus credit for any D-I transfers. Coefficients are fit on the 2024-25 and 2025-26 seasons. An optional high-school recruiting input adds at most a point or two.
  10. Predictions: projected tempo and efficiencies give a projected score. Win chance = Φ(margin / 11.9).

Back-test on the 2025-26 season (5,752 D-I games, each predicted with only earlier games, prior fit on 2024-25 only): average error 9.2 points on margin, 9.8 before mid-December. The winner was right 71.8% of the time.

Other columns

  • Luck: actual win % minus the win % expected from each game's efficiencies (Pythagorean exponent 10.25).
  • SOS: average current AdjEM of D-I opponents. NCSOS is the same for non-conference games.
  • Four Factors: eFG%, turnover %, offensive rebound %, FTA/FGA, for offense and defense.

NET+

The NCAA NET grades every game against the opponent's rank today, so a game's quadrant can change all season. NET+ grades each game once, on the day it is played, and never regrades it.

  • Every morning the model saves a ranking snapshot using only earlier games.
  • Each game takes its opponent rank, quadrant and wins-above-bubble value from the snapshot of its own date.
  • Quadrants use the NET cutoffs. Q1 is ranks 1-30 at home, 1-50 neutral and 1-75 away. Q2 is 31-75, 51-100 and 76-135. Q3 is 76-160, 101-200 and 136-240. Everything else is Q4.
  • WAB (wins above bubble) sums each result minus the chance that the #45 team would have won that same game, judged by the snapshot of the game's date.
  • NET+ score mirrors NET's two parts: half results (frozen WAB) and half efficiency (current AdjEM), each scaled to z-scores.
  • Live rank is the same formula with every game regraded by today's rankings, so you can see what freezing changes.

Snapshots use our AdjEM rank. When official NCAA NET rankings for a date are added to the data, NET+ freezes those instead from that date on.

Win chances and the watch score

Every upcoming game gets a projected score and a win chance from the ratings. The watch score (0-100) ranks games worth tuning in for: the two teams' average AdjEM sets the quality (−15 is 0, +30 is full marks), and that is scaled by how close the game should be, from 1 at a coin flip down to a quarter for a sure thing. The games page leads with the best six of the next game day.

Bracketology

The rest of the season is simulated 2,000 times each night.

  1. Each simulated season draws every team's true strength around its current AdjEM (standard deviation 3 points per 100 possessions), so a team can run hot or cold for a whole season. Game-to-game noise is reduced to match, so single-game predictions stay as accurate as on the games page.
  2. Every remaining scheduled game is played out. Wins above bubble for those games use today's bubble odds, the way NET+ would grade them on game day.
  3. Each conference tournament is played as single elimination, seeded by simulated conference record. Once tournaments start, teams that lost are out and a finished final locks in the auto bid.
  4. The field takes one automatic bid per conference, then the best at-large teams by a NET+-style selection score (half frozen wins above bubble, half AdjEM). The 76 are seeded on an S-curve. The 12 lowest-seeded auto bids and the 12 lowest-seeded at-large teams play a 12-game opening round, and the six winners from each group join the other 52 teams in the round of 64.
  5. The NCAA tournament is played on neutral courts, and the round-by-round odds are the share of simulations a team reached each round.

The projected bracket uses each league's likeliest tournament champion and the at-large teams with the best average selection score. It doesn't apply the committee's conference separation rules, and every league team is assumed to make its conference tournament. Early in the season it mostly reflects the ratings.

Player ratings

  • ORtg: Dean Oliver's individual offensive rating, points produced per 100 possessions used, from season totals (Basketball-Reference's formulas). It is left blank under 40 possessions used.
  • Usg%: share of team possessions a player uses (shots, free throws, turnovers) while on the floor. Min%: share of team minutes.
  • Off. value: (ORtg − national average efficiency) × Usg% × Min%, roughly the points per 100 team possessions a player adds over an average D-I offense using the same possessions. It rewards efficient, high-usage, heavy-minute players.
  • ARate: assists per teammate field goal while on the floor. TORate: turnovers per 100 possessions used. OR%/DR%: share of available rebounds grabbed while on the floor.

Team totals come from the same player box scores, and rates are blank under 10% of minutes.

Prediction tracker

Every D-I game is predicted with the ratings snapshot from the morning it was played, using only earlier games. These picks are rebuilt each night with the current code rather than logged, so they show what today's model would have said before tip-off, not a record of picks made earlier in the season with older settings.

Betting lines are the DraftKings spread and total shown in ESPN's game summary, as mirrored by sportsdataverse, at or near the closing line. A pick against the spread takes whichever side the model likes relative to the line; 52.4% is break-even at −110. The tracker also compares the model's average error with the line's on the same games.

Data

Box scores and schedules come from ESPN, through the free sportsdataverse/hoopR datasets. The site rebuilds every morning.
